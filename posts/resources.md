This post is a collection of resources that I have found useful for learning about optimal control and reinforcement learning. I will update this post as I find more resources.

## Basics

Some youtubers that I found useful to kickstart learning control:

[Brain Douglas](https://www.youtube.com/@BrianBDouglas): His control lectures could be a good starting point for learning the basics of control.

[Steve Brunton](https://www.youtube.com/@Eigensteve): UoW Prof. Steve Brunton has a series of lectures on control and dynamical systems as well as how it can be combined with machine learning. Highly recommend his control bootcamp series.

## Intermediate

Some introductory courses that could help you dive deeper into control:

[CMU Convex Optimization](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~ryantibs/convexopt/): optimization is everywhere in control. This course is a good starting point for learning about optimization, which I found more practical than the optimization course in Stanford Offered by Prof. Boyd. The course also comes with scribe, slides, quiz, and video lectures, which are very helpful for self-study.

[CMU Optimal Control](https://optimalcontrol.ri.cmu.edu/): A course offered by Prof. Zac, who is a leading expert in the field of optimal control. The content is more basic, coming with concrete examples and exercises. Comes with a course notebook, slides, and video lectures.
I have a course notebook [here](https://github.com/jc-bao/optimal-control-lecture-notes) and [homework solutions](https://github.com/jc-bao/CMU16-745-Optimal-Control-HW).

[Berkeley CS285](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/): most classic RL course and I still find it very useful.

[MIT Underactuated Robotics](http://underactuated.csail.mit.edu/underactuated.html): A book by Russ Tedrake that covers the basics of optimal control and reinforcement learning in the context of robotics. Online course available. The notebook is pretty good and could be a good tools for learning drake.

[MIT Robotic Manipulation](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/index.html): A book by Russ Tedrake that covers the basics of robotic manipulation. Mainly focus on solving for chain of rigid bodies and contact dynamics. The resource is also pretty good.

## Advanced

If you want to understand more about the theory behind control, here are some resources that I found useful:

[CMU Robotic Simulation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LiNgr1tz49I&list=PLZnJoM76RM6ItAfZIxJYNKdaR_BobleLY): another good course offered by Prof. Zac. If you want to dive deeper, understanding the simulation is very important.
